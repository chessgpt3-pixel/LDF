History 1 — Rise of Nationalism in Europe

1. French Revolution and the Idea of the Nation

  • Before the 19th century, Europe was organised into dynastic kingdoms rather than nation-states.
  • The 1789 French Revolution transferred sovereignty from monarch to citizens; promoted la patrie (fatherland) and le citoyen (citizen).
  • Napoleon introduced the Civil Code of 1804: equality before law, abolition of birth privileges, protection of property.

2. Liberalism & Zollverein

  • Liberalism: government by consent, constitutional rule, freedom of markets.
  • Zollverein customs union formed 1834 under Prussia — removed tariff barriers.

3. Treaty of Vienna 1815

  • Hosted by Duke Metternich; restored old conservative order after Napoleon's defeat.
  • Giuseppe Mazzini founded Young Italy and Young Europe — nations are natural units of mankind.

4. Age of Revolutions 1830–1848

  • 1830 — July Revolution in France; Belgium broke from Netherlands.
  • 1832 — Greece recognised independent (Treaty of Constantinople).
  • 1848 — Frankfurt Parliament drafted constitution; Friedrich Wilhelm IV rejected the crown.

5. Unification of Germany & Italy

  • Germany: Otto von Bismarck — "blood and iron" — wars vs Denmark, Austria, France. William I proclaimed Emperor Jan 1871 at Versailles.
  • Italy: Mazzini (idea), Count Cavour (diplomacy), Garibaldi (Red Shirts). 1861 Victor Emmanuel II proclaimed king.
★ BOARD FOCUS: Chronology, allegory identification, roles of Mazzini/Cavour/Garibaldi/Bismarck, Treaty of Vienna, Frankfurt Parliament, Balkan question.

History 2 — Nationalism in India

1. Satyagraha & Early Gandhi

  • Gandhi returned 1915; developed Satyagraha — non-violent resistance based on truth.
  • 1917 Champaran (indigo), Kheda (revenue), 1918 Ahmedabad (mill workers).

2. Rowlatt Act & Jallianwala Bagh

  • 1919 Rowlatt Act — detention without trial. Gandhi called nationwide hartal.
  • 13 April 1919 — General Dyer fired on peaceful gathering at Jallianwala Bagh.

3. Non-Cooperation Movement 1920–22

  • Khilafat Movement (Ali brothers) + Gandhi's Non-Cooperation united Hindus and Muslims.
  • Boycott of schools, courts, councils, foreign cloth. 1922 Chauri Chaura violence → Gandhi withdrew.

4. Civil Disobedience Movement

  • 1929 Lahore Congress under Nehru declared Purna Swaraj. Jan 26, 1930 = Independence Day.
  • 12 Mar–6 Apr 1930 Salt March: Sabarmati → Dandi.
  • 1931 Gandhi-Irwin Pact. 1932 Poona Pact with Dr B.R. Ambedkar.
★ BOARD FOCUS: Movement-wise objectives, participation, withdrawal causes, timeline 1915–1934.

History 3 — Making of a Global World

  • Silk Routes linked Asia–Europe–North Africa. Food crops crossed continents.
  • 19th century: flows of goods, labour, capital. Britain repealed Corn Laws. Indian indentured labourers went to Caribbean, Fiji, Mauritius.
  • 1929 Great Depression: bank failures, unemployment, falling farm prices.
  • 1944 Bretton Woods: created IMF & World Bank, stable exchange rates.

History 4 — Age of Industrialisation

  • Proto-industrialisation: rural household production organised by merchants before factories.
  • In India, gomasthas controlled weavers; Manchester cloth competed with Indian textiles.
  • Factories in Bombay & Bengal. Jamsetji Tata built modern industry.

History 5 — Print Culture & Modern World

  • Johannes Gutenberg — European press, 15th century. Gutenberg Bible.
  • Martin Luther's writings challenged Church authority.
  • 1780 James Augustus Hickey published Bengal Gazette.
  • 1878 Vernacular Press Act restricted Indian-language newspapers.
★ BOARD FOCUS: Gutenberg, religious debate, print & dissent, women readers, reform, colonial censorship.

Geography 1 — Resources and Development

  • Classifications: biotic/abiotic; renewable/non-renewable; potential/developed/stock/reserves.
  • Major soils: alluvial, black, red & yellow, laterite, arid, forest/mountain.
  • Alluvial soil: khadar (new) and bangar (old). Black soil retains moisture — good for cotton.
  • Soil erosion control: contour ploughing, terrace farming, strip cropping, shelter belts.

Geography 2 — Forest and Wildlife

  • IUCN categories: extinct, endangered, vulnerable, rare, endemic.
  • Wildlife Protection Act 1972. Project Tiger 1973.
  • Community conservation: sacred groves, Chipko Movement, Beej Bachao Andolan, Joint Forest Management.

Geography 3 — Water Resources

  • Multipurpose dams: Bhakra-Nangal, Tehri, Hirakud, Sardar Sarovar.
  • Rainwater harvesting: tankas, khadins, johads, guls/kuls, bamboo drip irrigation.
  • Narmada Bachao Andolan — social & environmental concerns about large dams.

Geography 4 — Agriculture

  • Seasons: Rabi (winter), Kharif (monsoon), Zaid (summer).
  • Green Revolution: HYV seeds, irrigation, fertilisers — raised productivity.

Geography 5 — Minerals and Energy

  • Metallic: ferrous (iron, manganese) and non-ferrous (bauxite → aluminium, mica).
  • Conventional: coal, petroleum, gas. Non-conventional: solar, wind, tidal, biogas, geothermal.

Geography 6 — Manufacturing Industries

  • Iron & steel centres: Jamshedpur, Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela.
  • 1984 Bhopal gas tragedy — industrial disaster case study.

Geography 7 — Lifelines of National Economy

  • Golden Quadrilateral: Delhi–Mumbai–Chennai–Kolkata.
  • Major ports: Kandla, Mumbai, JNPT, Chennai, Kolkata, Visakhapatnam.

Pol. Sci. 1 — Power Sharing

  • Belgium: equal central representation, community government, Brussels arrangement.
  • Sri Lanka: 1956 Sinhala Only Act → conflict with Tamil communities.
  • Prudential reason: reduces conflict. Moral reason: right to be consulted.
  • Forms: horizontal (legislature/exec/judiciary), vertical (levels of govt), social groups, political parties.

Pol. Sci. 2 — Federalism

  • Coming together: USA, Switzerland, Australia. Holding together: India, Spain, Belgium.
  • Union List (defence, foreign affairs), State List (police, agriculture), Concurrent List (education, forests).
  • 73rd & 74th Amendments strengthened local government (Panchayati Raj).

Pol. Sci. 3 — Gender, Religion & Caste

  • Communalism: religion used for political division or one group claiming superiority.
  • India is a secular state — no official religion, constitutional equality.

Pol. Sci. 4 — Political Parties

  • Functions: contest elections, offer policies, form govt, act as opposition, shape public opinion.
  • Challenges: lack of internal democracy, dynastic succession, money & muscle power.

Pol. Sci. 5 — Outcomes of Democracy

  • Provides accountable, responsive, legitimate government; allows peaceful correction of mistakes.
  • Promotes dignity and freedom; does not automatically eliminate poverty.
★ BOARD FOCUS: Comparisons, case studies, definitions, "why/how" questions especially important.

Economics 1 — Development

  • Per capita income = total income ÷ population. Helps compare but doesn't show inequality.
  • Other indicators: infant mortality, literacy, nutrition, public facilities access.
  • Human Development: health + education + standard of living.

Economics 2 — Sectors of Indian Economy

  • Primary: agriculture. Secondary: manufacturing. Tertiary: services.
  • Disguised unemployment: more workers than needed; removing some doesn't reduce output.
  • MGNREGA: legal guarantee of rural employment, 100 days per year.

Economics 3 — Money and Credit

  • Money solves double coincidence of wants problem.
  • Formal credit: banks, cooperatives (regulated). Informal: moneylenders, traders (high interest).
  • Self-help groups help borrowers access small loans.

Economics 4 — Globalisation & Indian Economy

  • MNCs organise production across countries via investment, partnerships, global supply chains.
  • 1991 economic reforms reduced trade & investment barriers. WTO provides rules.
  • Benefits uneven — small producers face strong competition.

Economics 5 — Consumer Rights

  • Rights: safety, information, choice, being heard, redressal, consumer education.
  • Quality marks: ISI, Hallmark, Agmark.
